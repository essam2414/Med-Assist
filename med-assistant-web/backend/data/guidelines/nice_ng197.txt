NICE Guideline NG197: Shared Decision Making - Essential Clinical Recommendations

DEFINITION:
Shared decision making is a collaborative process that involves a person and their healthcare professional working together to reach a joint decision about care. It involves choosing tests and treatments based both on evidence and on the person's individual preferences, beliefs and values.

CORE CLINICAL PRINCIPLES (SECTION 1.2):
- Support shared decision making before, during, and after discussions.
- Ask the person if they want to involve family, friends, carers, or advocates.
- Agree an 'agenda' at the start of each discussion; say how long it will last.
- Explain healthcare aims of each option and discuss how they align with the person's priorities and wider goals.
- Openly discuss risks, benefits, and consequences, including the choice of no treatment or no change.
- Clarify what the person hopes to gain and discuss any misconceptions.
- Use 'chunk and check': deliver information in manageable segments and check understanding using the 'teach back' technique.
- Record decisions along with details of what the person said was important to them (values and preferences).
- Write clinical letters TO the patient rather than to their healthcare professional.

COMMUNICATING RISKS AND CONSEQUENCES (SECTION 1.4):
- Discuss risks in the context of each person's life and what matters to them.
- Use good-quality patient decision aids, pictographs, or icon arrays.
- Personalise information on risks and make uncertainty clear.
- Use absolute risk (e.g., 1 in 1000) rather than relative risk (e.g., risk doubles).
- Use natural frequencies (e.g., 10 in 100) rather than percentages (10%).
- Use consistent denominators when comparing risks (e.g., 7 in 100 vs 20 in 100).
- Use both positive and negative framing (e.g., 97 out of 100 succeed, 3 out of 100 do not).

KEY TECHNIQUES:
- Teach Back: Confirming understanding by getting people to 'teach back' what has been discussed.
- Three-talk model: Introducing choice, Describing options, and Helping people explore preferences.
