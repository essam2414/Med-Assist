NICE Guideline CG95: Recent-onset Chest Pain of Suspected Cardiac Origin - Essential Recommendations

ACUTE CHEST PAIN - INITIAL ASSESSMENT (SECTION 1.2):
- Check if currently in pain. If pain-free, check when the last episode was (especially within last 12h).
- Assess for Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS) symptoms:
    * Pain lasting >15 minutes.
    * Pain associated with nausea, vomiting, marked sweating, or breathlessness.
    * New onset pain or abrupt deterioration of stable angina.
- Do NOT use response to GTN to make a diagnosis.
- Do NOT assess symptoms differently for men vs. women or different ethnic groups.

EMERGENCY REFERRAL & IMMEDIATE MANAGEMENT:
- Refer to hospital as an emergency if ACS is suspected AND:
    * Patient currently has chest pain OR
    * Patient is pain-free but had pain in last 12h and resting 12-lead ECG is abnormal/unavailable.
- Immediate treatment:
    * Offer pain relief (GTN or intravenous morphine if myocardial infarction suspected).
    * Single loading dose of 300mg Aspirin (unless allergic).
    * Monitor oxygen saturation (SpO2); only offer oxygen if SpO2 < 94% (or 88-92% for COPD).

STABLE CHEST PAIN - CLINICAL ASSESSMENT (SECTION 1.3):
- Documentation: Age, sex, pain characteristics (location, radiation, severity, duration, frequency, provoking/relieving factors), history of CVD/angina, and risk factors.
- Anginal Pain Definition:
    1. Constricting discomfort in chest, neck, shoulders, jaw, or arms.
    2. Precipitated by physical exertion.
    3. Relieved by rest or GTN within ~5 minutes.
- Classification:
    * Typical Angina: Presence of all 3 features.
    * Atypical Angina: Presence of 2 of 3 features.
    * Non-anginal Pain: 1 or 0 features.
- Factors making stable angina UNLIKELY: Pain is continuous/very prolonged, unrelated to activity, brought on by breathing, or associated with dizziness/tingling/swallowing difficulty.

DIAGNOSTIC TESTING:
- 12-lead resting ECG should be taken promptly. Normal ECG does NOT exclude ACS.
- High-sensitivity troponin tests (I or T) are used in hospital to diagnose/rule out myocardial infarction.
- Consider other life-threatening causes: Pulmonary embolism, aortic dissection, pneumonia, pneumothorax.
