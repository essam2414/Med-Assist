NICE Guideline NG193: Chronic Pain (Primary and Secondary) - Essential Recommendations

DEFINITIONS:
- Chronic Pain: Pain that persists or recurs for more than 3 months.
- Chronic Primary Pain: Pain with no clear underlying condition, or where the impact is out of proportion to any observable injury/disease (e.g., fibromyalgia, complex regional pain syndrome).
- Chronic Secondary Pain: Pain caused by an underlying condition (e.g., osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis).

ASSESSMENT PRINCIPLES (SECTION 1.1):
- Offer a person-centred assessment to identify factors contributing to pain and its impact on life.
- Foster collaborative and supportive relationships.
- Explore how pain affects lifestyle, activities, sleep, psychological wellbeing, and social relationships.
- Be sensitive to socioeconomic, cultural, and ethnic backgrounds.
- Discuss expectations, futures, and outcome of treatments.
- For people aged 16-25, consider age-related symptoms and family dynamics.
- Acknowledge that living with pain is distressing.

CARE AND SUPPORT PLANNING:
- Discuss priorities, abilities, and goals.
- Explain evidence for benefits, risks, and uncertainties of all management options.
- Create a care and support plan based on priorities and preferred approaches.
- Re-evaluate the diagnosis (especially primary vs. secondary) if the presentation changes.

NON-PHARMACOLOGICAL MANAGEMENT (SECTION 1.2):
- Offer supervised group exercise programmes.
- Consider Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) or Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT).
- Consider a single course of acupuncture or dry needling (max 5 hours professional time).
- Do NOT offer TENS, ultrasound, or interferential therapy for chronic primary pain.

PHARMACOLOGICAL MANAGEMENT:
- Consider antidepressants (amitriptyline, citalopram, duloxetine, fluoxetine, paroxetine, or sertraline) for people aged 18+ to manage primary pain. Explain these help with quality of life, sleep, and distress even without depression.
- Do NOT initiate antiepileptics (gabapentinoids), antipsychotics, benzodiazepines, NSAIDs, opioids, or paracetamol for chronic primary pain.
- If already taking these medicines, review via shared decision making; explain lack of evidence and discuss withdrawal/reduction plans if benefit is low.
- For 16-17 year olds, seek specialist advice before starting antidepressants.
