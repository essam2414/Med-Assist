NICE Guideline CG138: Patient Experience in Adult NHS Services - Essential Recommendations

KNOWING THE PATIENT AS AN INDIVIDUAL (SECTION 1.1):
- Acknowledge the patient's individuality and the unique way they experience their condition.
- Address physical or learning disabilities, or language barriers (e.g., sight, speech, hearing problems).
- Take into account domestic, social, and work situations and previous healthcare experiences.
- Listen to health beliefs, concerns, and preferences without making assumptions based on appearance.
- Discuss the need for psychological, social, spiritual, and/or financial support if appropriate.

RESPECT AND COMMUNICATION (SECTION 1.2 & 1.5):
- Treat patients with respect, kindness, dignity, compassion, and honesty.
- Respect the right to confidentiality; do not discuss the patient in their presence without involving them.
- Raise and discuss sensitive issues (sexual activity, continence, end-of-life care) that patients may not bring up themselves.
- Non-judgementally listen to and discuss fears or concerns.
- Use open-ended questions to encourage discussion.
- Summarise information at the end of a consultation and check understanding of the most important points.
- Position yourself at the same level as the patient and maintain eye contact if culturally appropriate.
- Avoid jargon and define unfamiliar words.

TAILORING SERVICES AND SHARED DECISION MAKING (SECTION 1.3):
- Adopt an individualised approach tailored to needs, Coexisting conditions, and preferences.
- Allow adequate time so discussions do not feel rushed.
- Accept that patients may have different views from healthcare professionals about the balance of risks and benefits.
- Respect the patient's right to decide NOT to have treatment (informed refusal).
- Support the patient in their choice, even if you disagree.
- Clarify whether and how family members/carers should be involved in key decisions.

ACTIVE PARTICIPATION AND INFORMATION (SECTION 1.5):
- Give both oral and written information in an accessible format.
- Explore the patient's preferences regarding the level and type of information they want.
- Ask if the patient wants to be accompanied or would like to take notes/audio recordings.
- Give patients the support they need to maintain their independence.
